# Transitions between areas

These eight transition archetypes make distinct facets part of one traversable world. Use them as bounded route segments or site approaches, not extra towns or dungeons. Follow [the area guide](../areas-guide.md) for ownership and feature gating.

Every transition preserves a connected ordinary route, communicates the nearby area's identity, and belongs to one shared plan across chunk boundaries. Place clues in both travel directions. Transition width should follow the actual composition rather than a universal fixed distance.

## Survey Road

Area ID: `transition_survey_road`. Band: Common. Build: Layout. Placement: any biome between ordinary country and an old survey, archive, or administrative site.

Identity: a current road follows some of the old atlas workers' route.

Build: gradually introduce worked stone, aligned roadside rocks, cleared approach ground, and a view of the actual entrance. Ordinary vegetation remains outside the route.

Life and discovery: an assigned local record links a placed mark, a Milewarden's future hint, and a future survey book. A general rumor should not point to an unplaced marker.

First version: existing road and stone forms with composition. Survey inscriptions and readable milestones need art and interaction support.

## Rootline

Area ID: `transition_rootline`. Band: Distinctive. Build: Art. Placement: ordinary Greenwood entering Mycelium Downs, Pale Grove, or Rootbound Ward.

Identity: changes in growth and old pruning practices indicate that the next community treats the forest differently.

Build: gradually replace ordinary trunks or understory with the target area's distinctive growth. Reserve a dry open lane and a clearing that first reveals its landmark.

Life and discovery: a cut root, a pruning mark, or an actual fungal specimen can be the subject of a future forester's explanation.

First version: clustered ordinary growth with a few new target-area assets. Living roots, grasping vines, and spore effects require mechanics.

## Sandglass Margin

Area ID: `transition_sandglass_margin`. Band: Distinctive. Build: Art. Placement: Amber Wastes entering Glass Heath or Amber Garden.

Identity: ordinary mineral fragments become unfamiliar, while traders continue using a repaired practical road.

Build: increase glasslike or amber outcrops gradually, keep rough opaque walking surfaces, and reveal the district's principal formation across an open space.

Life and discovery: a repair shelter can contain a future mender's sample record tied to the actual local material.

First version: new material assets along an ordinary road. Sharp-ground damage, shattering, and preservation effects need systems.

## Warmstone Verge

Area ID: `transition_warmstone_verge`. Band: Distinctive. Build: Layout. Placement: Frostlands approaching Winter Hearth or a less exposed neighboring district.

Identity: travelers encounter signs of deliberate shelter before reaching the town's hearths.

Build: pine windbreaks, warm stone tones, torch-marked approaches, and a protected clearing. Use actual biome variation where it exists; do not invent a whole warm climate from a color change.

Life and discovery: a future scarf note or wrapped bell explains the community's habits. Ordinary torches guide the return route.

First version: existing pines, torches, and palette variation. Heat fields, melting snow, and cold protection require mechanics.

## Cloudfall Steps

Area ID: `transition_cloudfall_steps`. Band: Exceptional. Build: Art. Placement: a connected upland approach to grounded Cloudmoor terrain.

Identity: travelers climb reachable stone terraces as the materials and surrounding growth become pale.

Build: gentle steps or a winding pass, low cloudlike art beyond route edges, and a stable view of the destination. Include ordinary landings and a reachable reverse route.

Life and discovery: a future observer's note records the actual climb and distinguishes it from claims of floating roads.

First version: grounded terraces and pale environmental assets. Walkable clouds, wind lifts, and flights between suspended platforms need systems.

## Boundary Common

Area ID: `transition_boundary_common`. Band: Common. Build: Layout. Placement: a road between ordinary and themed settlements, or between two distinct facets.

Identity: neighbors maintain neutral meeting ground, exchange goods, and compare incompatible versions of local history.

Build: an open rest court beside a through-road. Place modest containers or banners from both nearby styles, with each material tied to a real neighboring area.

Life and discovery: future travelers discuss trade, a shared bridge, or an unresolved border custom. The court is not a sanctuary unless explicitly implemented as one.

First version: existing props and an open roadside composition. Gatherings, trade, rest services, and reputation outcomes need support.

## Old Causeway

Area ID: `transition_old_causeway`. Band: Distinctive. Build: Layout. Placement: an actual valid bridge or dry crossing near a wet district or old works.

Identity: people repaired an ancient route whose original builders crossed the same obstacle.

Build: one connected crossing, old stone cues at both ends, and contrasting present-day repairs. Bank paths must reconnect to accessible ground.

Life and discovery: a future repair ledger connects the crossing with a Waterworks or Fungal Cistern. The author can dispute its age without disputing the visible crossing.

First version: existing bridge geometry with repaired-stone art. Moving platforms, water levels, swimming, and flood damage require systems.

## Sleeper's Verge

Area ID: `transition_sleepers_verge`. Band: Exceptional. Build: Systems. Placement: ordinary country approaching Dream Market or Sleepers Gallery.

Identity: familiar objects carry repeated dream motifs, then give way to a community with a distinctive storytelling tradition.

Build: stable route markers, painted repeated shapes, mismatched grounded furnishings, and one clearly recognizable destination. Keep the first version's navigation entirely physical.

Life and discovery: a future dream diary mentions an actual painted marker. Reports of walking the route while asleep remain testimony.

First version: static art along an ordinary path. Shifting paths, sleep conditions, perception effects, and altered map states require systems and saved continuity.

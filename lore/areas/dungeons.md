# Dungeon families

These twenty-four families apply to the existing ruin sites. Follow [the area guide](../areas-guide.md) for persistent identity, finite floors, rewards, and staged implementation.

The three existing themes currently cycle by depth. Their entries below describe how they could also become family identities. Every other family is proposed content. The current generator's rectangular rooms provide an initial shell; each family needs room roles, prop placement, and a recognizable spatial pattern.

All first versions preserve the current warden-clear gate and final chest loop. Reserve an unobstructed entry, return stairs, gate or chest, and enemy spawn clearance. The listed existing enemy kinds are temporary real encounter choices, not newly implemented themed monsters.

For long dungeons, repeat a motif with varied rooms and occasional contrasting chambers. For one-floor dungeons, use a compact form of the same history and room vocabulary. Floors need not contain every room role at once.

## The Ashen Crypt

Area ID: `dungeon_ashen_crypt`. Band: Common. Build: Layout. Placement: any biome; favor old burial districts and Cinder Verge.

Identity: burial chambers preserve duty workings after their attendants and occupants are gone.

Build: entry vestibule, paired burial rooms, attendant store, and a larger memorial room. Use ash-gray stone, ordinary warm torches, and sparse household remains. Alternate wide burial rooms with short corridors.

Encounters and discovery: prefer Crypt Skeletons while permitting the other existing kinds. Place the future Bed Counter's Ledger in an attendant room tied to the site's history.

First version: reuse the current crypt palette and floor assets with distinct room dressing. Sarcophagi and funerary furniture need art. Peaceful release of skeletons remains a future mechanic.

## The Violet Sanctum

Area ID: `dungeon_violet_sanctum`. Band: Common. Build: Layout. Placement: any biome with an old practitioner school or survey institution.

Identity: defensive lessons continue in a school that has lost its teachers and its timetable.

Build: registration room, practice chambers, preparation stores, and an examination hall. Use violet accents and clear firing lanes broken by reachable cover arrangements. Keep paths around every obstacle.

Encounters and discovery: prefer Sanctum Invokers. A future lesson sheet explains one narrow exercise and names an instructor from this site's recorded past.

First version: reuse the current sanctum palette and ordinary geometry. Desks, inscriptions, spell diagrams, and tutors are later content. No examination password opens the current gate.

## The Verdant Tomb

Area ID: `dungeon_verdant_tomb`. Band: Common. Build: Layout. Placement: Greenwood; Pale Grove; damp districts in other biomes.

Identity: roots have entered memorial rooms where lingering impressions still mistake arrivals for lost companions.

Build: entrance court, memorial alcoves, root-crossed passage, and an open remembrance hall. Keep roots as readable edge decoration before adding solid root obstacles.

Encounters and discovery: prefer Tomb Shades. A future private letter records an ordinary visit to the memorial before its decline.

First version: reuse the verdant palette, stone floors, and room shell. Root assets and memorial furniture improve identity. Apparitions and memory reconstruction need systems.

## Survey Cellars

Area ID: `dungeon_survey_cellars`. Band: Common. Build: Layout. Placement: near a Milewarden route, marked grove, or Archive Steps town.

Identity: a working cellar stored maps, tools, and spare boundary stones. One survey ended without its final report.

Build: receiving chamber, long measurement lane, side stores, and a records room. Align some rooms along a clear main spine; let optional branches contain storage evidence.

Encounters and discovery: existing skeletons and invokers fit surviving duty workings. A future travel note connects a placed surface survey mark to this particular office.

First version: containers, stone floors, and a new room-role arrangement. Survey tools and map tables need art; submitting reports is a future interaction.

## Quarry Depths

Area ID: `dungeon_quarry_depths`. Band: Common. Build: Layout. Placement: Quarry Town; accessible rock faces; Colossus Rest margins.

Identity: workers opened an extraction site and discovered chambers belonging to an older builder.

Build: work forecourt, narrow extraction branches, supply room, and a clearly older stone chamber. Contrast rough work edges with regular masonry. Preserve broad turning points for combat.

Encounters and discovery: existing skeletons can be remnants of former labor duties; shades fit the older rooms. A future ledger records the first wall the workers did not recognize.

First version: existing stone and container forms. Tools, mine supports, ore, mining, and collapse hazards need additional support.

## Bandit Storehouse

Area ID: `dungeon_bandit_storehouse`. Band: Common. Build: Layout. Placement: roads outside sanctuary settlements in any biome.

Identity: several generations of thieves reused an old storehouse. Its latest occupants abandoned it after disturbing the buried wardens.

Build: concealed entry court, crowded store rooms with clear lanes, sleeping chamber, and an older vault beneath the ordinary storage. Use mismatched containers and repairs.

Encounters and discovery: existing enemies belong to the disturbed older rooms. Future living bandits need separate definitions. A future inventory lists belongings whose owners can remain unknown.

First version: container-heavy dressing and old stone architecture. Locks, theft, traps, bandit factions, and stolen-item quests are later mechanics.

## Waterworks

Area ID: `dungeon_waterworks`. Band: Common. Build: Layout. Placement: near actual rivers, bridge towns, or old municipal supply sites.

Identity: a settlement's buried maintenance works outlasted the city that first used them.

Build: service entry, dry inspection spine, reservoir galleries, tool stores, and a valve chamber. Put decorative channels outside the continuous playable floor until water collision is supported underground.

Encounters and discovery: existing skeletons and invokers fit abandoned maintenance instructions. A future repair book records how neighboring communities shared the supply.

First version: dry rooms, pots, containers, and stone channels as scenery. Flood levels, moving water, valves, and swimming require systems.

## Root Chapel

Area ID: `dungeon_root_chapel`. Band: Distinctive. Build: Art. Placement: Greenwood; Rootbound Ward; Pale Grove.

Identity: a sanctuary of an unresolved local faith was gradually occupied by roots. Its present caretakers remember customs more clearly than doctrine.

Build: processional corridor, side offerings, circular-looking chamber within the grid, and a root-framed memorial room. Roots direct attention toward the final room without becoming impassable curtains.

Encounters and discovery: existing shades fit lingering impressions. A future caretaker's note distinguishes the old building from the younger tree.

First version: root sculptures and chapel props with normal movement. A responsive tree, divine powers, and offering-based completion require explicit design.

## Fungal Cistern

Identity: fungi colonized damp storage chambers used by drainage workers. Residents still recognize some useful growths.

Build: dry service walk, broad colony chambers, specimen stores, and an old water hall. Cluster fungi at room edges and reserve contrasting open floors.

## Glass Labyrinth

Area ID: `dungeon_glass_labyrinth`. Band: Exceptional. Build: Art. Placement: Glass Heath; Glasswater; a recorded glazier institution.

Identity: an old school built translucent sample walls around perfectly ordinary service corridors. Later damage confused visitors about which surfaces remain solid.

Build: ringlike routes with shortcuts, opaque floor lanes, clear solid wall bases, and one broad faceted hall. Readable collision takes priority over realistic transparency.

Encounters and discovery: existing invokers and shades suit the abandoned school. A future sample record explains a placed glass formation outside.

First version: new glasslike walls and an actual loop layout. Reflection puzzles, false passages, breakable walls, and light routing require mechanics.

## Sand Archive

## Mask Theatre

Area ID: `dungeon_mask_theatre`. Band: Distinctive. Build: Art. Placement: Maskmakers Square; any district with a recorded theatrical history.

Identity: performers once used an underground theatre for plays about the Dawn. Their competing versions of the story survive in backstage notes.

Build: foyer, broad stage chamber, parallel backstage route, wardrobe stores, and rehearsal room. Let the main stage reveal scenery first glimpsed from a side passage.

Encounters and discovery: existing shades and invokers fit lingering performances and old stage workings. A future cast list contains ordinary grudges beside disputed history.

First version: mask, curtain, and stage assets. Possessed costumes, changing identity, scripted performances, and audience mechanics need support.

## Banquet Cellars

Area ID: `dungeon_banquet_cellars`. Band: Distinctive. Build: Art. Placement: Breadhearth; Grain Town; a large former feast shelter.

Identity: kitchens prepared for a gathering that never happened. Later households used the accessible stores until something old began defending them.

Build: receiving stores, preparation rooms, wide oven hall, narrow service return, and a long final dining chamber. Give the kitchen and banquet different proportions.

Encounters and discovery: existing skeletons retain service instructions; shades fit the empty dining room. A future kitchen memoir disputes the canceled guest list.

First version: ovens, utensils, tables, and solid foodlike decoration. Edible walls, animated meals, cooking, and heat hazards require mechanics.

Identity: households planted small underground gardens beside written memories of the dead. Later roots joined gardens whose families never knew each other.

## The Unclosed Office

Area ID: `dungeon_unclosed_office`. Band: Distinctive. Build: Layout. Placement: Archive Steps; Survey Cellars districts; any recorded atlas administration site.

Identity: an atlas office continues to wait for the report that will finish its work. Its remaining procedures disagree about who can authorize closure.

Build: receiving room, long filing galleries, short approval offices, waiting court, and a final assembly hall. Use misaligned administrative additions to distinguish it from a simple school.

Encounters and discovery: existing skeletons and invokers repeat administrative duties. Instructions for the Final Atlas belongs in a future records room. Reserve the Last Usher for one uniquely tracked story, not every office.

First version: existing stone, containers, banners, and differentiated room roles. Paper furniture, working bureaucracy, unique bosses, and alternate completion require support.

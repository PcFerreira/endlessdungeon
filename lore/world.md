# World foundation

The Endless World, the collapse of the multiverse, and the player's guild membership are established setting pillars. The passages below guide the voices of travelers, guild members, and found books. Further local history remains open for development.

## A road that keeps going

Once there were countless worlds. When the multiverse collapsed, their lands, peoples, and wonders came together in one infinite world. Its inhabitants call it the Endless World.

A road can leave a village of timber houses and reach towers built from stone that remembers another sky. Beyond those towers, someone grows beans. The neighbors have learned to trade long before they have agreed on how their ancestors arrived.

Travelers have crossed this world for generations. Every serious expedition has found another valley, another settlement, and another stretch of road. Some have returned with maps too large to unfold indoors. There is always more country beyond the last sheet.

Cities gather around sanctuary fountains. Beyond their streets lie forests, meadows, dry wastes, and frozen country. Beneath old arches, stairs descend into places built by people whose names survive more often on curses than monuments.

A traveler might spend the morning fighting a crypt guardian and the afternoon helping a clay neighbor reach a high shelf. Both events matter to the people involved.

## The Unfinished Dawn

People call the collapse the Unfinished Dawn. The name comes from accounts of skies that brightened for days while unfamiliar lands appeared beyond doorways and hills. Families kept the names of their old homes. Some still use them for the places where they live now.

During the Dawn, a league of rulers and magicians tried to complete a final atlas. They meant to give every place a permanent position, every road a destination, and every border an owner. Their orders survive in buildings where nobody has answered them for generations.

The atlas failed. The worlds came together, and the roads kept going. Nobody agrees on what began the collapse or what the league hoped to save.

Present towns and routes stay in their locations. New country lies beyond familiar maps. A family's story of another world does not mean their village moves when they close the door.

Some districts preserve the building traditions, creatures, and materials of a former world. Others have changed through centuries of shared life. A traveler can recognize a place's kinship with another without knowing the name of the world they once belonged to.

## Your place among the Milewardens

You belong to the Milewardens, a traveling guild that maps the Endless World and catalogues its mysteries. Guild members copy inscriptions, listen to neighbors, examine old workings, and bring back accounts of places their companions have yet to reach.

Your field journal begins where someone else's map ends. A town's name, a broken stair, a keeper's warning, and a creature's habits all deserve a place on its pages. Sometimes the most useful thing you bring home is the route that brought you back.

The guild keeps unfinished maps in every chapter house. Members add what they have seen and leave room for what they have only heard. Two accounts can sit beside one another until someone walks the road between them.

## What people know and what they dispute

| Subject | Established lore | Belief or unresolved question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Geography | The Endless World is a single infinite world formed by the collapse of the multiverse | Which former world a particular district came from |
| The Dawn | The collapse joined formerly separate worlds; the league's final atlas failed | What began the collapse and how the atlas working was involved |
| The player | A member of the Milewardens, mapping places and cataloguing mysteries | Their earlier journeys and personal reasons for joining |
| Fountains | Sanctuary towns offer protection and recovery | Whether all fountains share one source |
| Ruins | Old workings and hostile wardens survive underground | Whether every warden was once a person |
| Object life | Some crafted vessels became people | Whether this can still happen intentionally |
| Gods | People practice different faiths | The true pantheon, divine history, and afterlife |

Keep the recovery and protection offered by sanctuary cities consistent with gameplay. The fountain's deeper explanation remains folklore. Resurrection theology is open; the current respawn mechanic alone does not settle how death works for everyone.

## Peoples and belonging

Humans, elves, dwarves, and goblins provide familiar fantasy roots. Towns contain mixed households, trades, and traditions. Choose an individual's values from their history and community.

Potfolk are a proposed people descended from enchanted vessels that continued working after the Dawn. They mend cracks, commission new handles, and argue about whether a repaired body changes a person's identity. They are citizens and travelers. Making them visible requires new character support.

Other unusual peoples can be developed later. Write their daily needs and relationships before assigning a kingdom or a combat role. Keep player ancestry choices open until the game supports them.

## Author notes

Guild membership is the player's place in the fiction. It does not add ranks, dues, faction reputation, guild services, or a new quest system. Those features need separate implementation. A town may contain a local chapter without sharing a named leader with every other town.

Use the collapse to explain the world's different visual traditions. Keep each place coherent with its residents, materials, and history; the premise does not require random mixtures of asset styles within a biome or town. Keep asset packs, generator rules, and source reliability labels out of dialogue and readable passages. The research files retain their original proposals and source context; this page states the adopted setting.

## Questions for our next lore sessions

The setting can grow around the people who live here. Decide whether the atlas league was frightened, ambitious, or trying to prevent something worse. Develop a few local faiths before defining a universal pantheon. Explore which memories of former worlds survived in particular households.

An early story seed: a Milewarden finds a road recorded in the old atlas that has never existed on the surface. The Unclosed Office insists its travelers are overdue. Reserve this as a future story, with no current quest promises.

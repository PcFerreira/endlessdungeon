# World foundation

Status: draft proposal. Placement: author reference; reveal fragments through NPCs, ruins, and books rather than displaying this whole document to players.

## A road that keeps going

Travelers have crossed the Endless Realm for generations. Every serious expedition has found another valley, another settlement, and another stretch of road. Some have returned with maps too large to unfold indoors. Nobody has returned with an agreed account of the world's edge.

Cities gather around sanctuary fountains. Beyond their streets lie forests, meadows, dry wastes, and frozen country. Beneath old arches, stairs descend into places built by people whose names survive more often on curses than monuments.

A traveler might spend the morning fighting a crypt guardian and the afternoon helping a clay neighbor reach a high shelf. Both events matter to the people involved.

## The Unfinished Dawn

Long ago, a league of rulers and magicians tried to draw a final, complete atlas. Their great working would assign every place its permanent position, every road its destination, and every border its rightful owner.

The working failed. Witness accounts describe a dawn that lasted several days, stairways appearing beneath public buildings, and household enchantments continuing after their makers died. Later generations call this event the Unfinished Dawn.

Within this draft, the working happened and failed. Its exact relationship to the world's endlessness remains unresolved. No narrator should declare that it created infinity. The disaster was magical; Earth, nuclear war, and Ooo's history are not part of this setting.

Present towns and routes stay in their locations. New country lies beyond familiar maps. A tale about a moving town needs its own local explanation and future gameplay support.

The [Area guide](areas-guide.md) proposes stable local facets with very different materials and customs inside this shared geography. Their origins can differ. The catalogue preserves the open question of whether the world ever consisted of separate worlds.

## What people know and what they dispute

| Subject | Reliable within the draft | Belief or unresolved question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Geography | Travel reveals more country beyond mapped regions | Whether there is a literal edge |
| The Dawn | A failed atlas working damaged old settlements | Whether the working caused endlessness or exposed it |
| Fountains | Sanctuary towns offer protection and recovery | Whether all fountains share one source |
| Ruins | Old workings and hostile wardens survive underground | Whether every warden was once a person |
| Object life | Some crafted vessels became people | Whether this can still happen intentionally |
| Gods | People practice different faiths | The true pantheon, divine history, and afterlife |

Keep the recovery and protection offered by sanctuary cities consistent with gameplay. The fountain's deeper explanation remains folklore. Resurrection theology is open; the current respawn mechanic alone does not settle how death works for everyone.

## Peoples and belonging

Humans, elves, dwarves, and goblins provide familiar fantasy roots. Towns contain mixed households, trades, and traditions. Choose an individual's values from their history and community.

Potfolk are a proposed people descended from enchanted vessels that continued working after the Dawn. They mend cracks, commission new handles, and argue about whether a repaired body changes a person's identity. They are citizens and travelers. Making them visible requires new character support.

Other unusual peoples can be developed later. Write their daily needs and relationships before assigning a kingdom or a combat role. Keep player ancestry choices open until the game supports them.

## Groups found along the roads

| Group | What they do | Recurring disagreement | Intended placement |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Hearthkeepers | Tend sanctuary fountains and welcome travelers | Whether dangerous relics should enter a town | Fountain NPCs; notices and household books |
| Milewardens | Survey roads, record settlements, and maintain route records | Whether damaged old maps belong in public circulation | Roadside NPCs; atlas flavor and travel journals |
| The Unclosed Office | Surviving remnants of the old atlas administration | Its instructions assume the Dawn is still pending | Ruin inscriptions, books, and enemy speech |
| The Small Repairs | A loose association of menders and craftspeople | Who owns a newly awakened tool | Future workshops, Potfolk NPCs, and repair manuals |

These are recurring organizations, not implemented reputation systems. A town may contain a local chapter without having the same named leader as every other town.

## Questions for our next lore sessions

The setting can grow around the people who live here. Decide whether the Dawn's architects were frightened, ambitious, or trying to prevent something worse. Develop a few local faiths before defining a universal pantheon. Choose how strange the wider world should become beyond the four current biomes.

An early story seed: a Milewarden finds a road recorded in the old atlas that has never existed on the surface. The Unclosed Office insists its travelers are overdue. Reserve this as a future story, with no current quest promises.

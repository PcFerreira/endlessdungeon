## A Cup for Whoever Comes Next

Book ID: `book_waiting_cup`. Author: a Hearthkeeper's household, copied anonymously. Placement: future sanctuary shelf or fountain notice. Reliability: local custom, not universal history.

> We used to set an extra place at supper for travelers. Then we had three travelers and only one spare plate. Grandmother said the custom needed improvement.
>
> Now we keep a clean cup beside the door. Whoever comes next may use it. Whoever comes after that may wash it.
>
> Grandmother called this hospitality. Grandfather called it a successful division of labor. They agreed for nearly sixty years.

## Instructions for the Final Atlas

Book ID: `book_final_atlas`. Author: the Unclosed Office, unsigned circular. Placement: future readable document in any old ruin, with preference for violet sanctums. Reliability: genuine old instruction; its predictions failed.

> Each road shall receive a destination. Each settlement shall receive a place. Each border shall receive an owner.
>
> Unregistered valleys must be reported before dawn. Reports received after dawn will be considered impossible and returned to their sender.
>
> Please refrain from moving during the completion ceremony. The Office cannot accept responsibility for persons entered under the wrong country.
>
> Tomorrow, the map will be finished.

## Notes on Keeping Water

Book ID: `book_water_notes`. Author: caravan mender Sef Dapple. Placement: future Amber Wastes workshop or caravan shelter. Reliability: practical advice with unverified personal observations.

> Shade the jar before you shade your boots. Boots complain less expensively.
>
> Repair a crack before the journey. A cup repaired halfway across the wastes becomes a meeting of everybody's opinions.
>
> I have seen a rain jar emptied by a careless child and by a very careful adult. The child admitted it sooner.

## The Bed Counter's Ledger

Book ID: `book_bed_ledger`. Author: an unnamed attendant before the Dawn. Placement: future crypt room furnishing, paired with a local skeleton history. Reliability: personal record; the final entry's meaning is uncertain.

> Twelve beds. Twelve clean blankets. Eleven cups.
>
> Apprentice took a cup downstairs. Remind him tomorrow.
>
> Twelve beds. Thirteen blankets. Someone expects company.
>
> Instructions arrived from the Office. We are to keep everyone in their assigned places until the ceremony ends.
>
> Twelve beds. Twelve blankets. No one sleeping.
>
> Count again in the morning.

## A Letter Kept Warm

Book ID: `book_warm_letter`. Author: Iven, recipient unnamed. Placement: future Frostlands travel cache or inn shelf. Reliability: personal testimony, with no confirmed surviving sender or recipient.

> I charged the scarf at the inn. It will keep its warmth until morning. There is a loose thread beside the clasp, so pull gently.
>
> You said I never remember practical things. I remembered the scarf, the bread, and your terrible little spoon.
>
> I meant to remember a better goodbye. You will have to accept this one until I return.

## On the Ownership of Handles

Book ID: `book_handles`. Author: Pella of the Blue Handle. Placement: future Potfolk household or repair workshop; copies can circulate as books before Pella appears. Reliability: the author's opinion and account of her own life.

> My first handle was chosen by the woman who made me. It was a good handle. I used it for thirty years.
>
> When it broke, the mender asked what kind I wanted. Nobody had asked before.
>
> I chose blue. My maker would have preferred green. I think we could have discussed it over tea.
>
> I would have poured.

## Procedural book families

Preserve the complete authored passages above. Generate additional books from a record describing a particular place and event, rather than shuffling their paragraphs.

| Family | Select and store | Passage pattern | Placement |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Travel note | Author, visited city, actual local ruin, one observation | Arrival; observation; personal response | Future roadside cache or inn |
| Household manual | Author, one supported fictional enchantment, its limit | Instruction; remembered mistake; margin note | Future home or workshop |
| Office circular | Issuing office, duty, obsolete assumption | Order; exception; failed prediction | Future ruin archive |
| Private letter | Sender, recipient, relationship, one shared object | Practical news; personal detail; unfinished intention | Future travel cache or tomb |

Store these choices with the book's stable identity. Multiple copies of the same edition must share its text. A new edition can disagree with an older one if the record identifies the change.

Example travel-note template:

> I reached {city_name} with mud on both sleeves. From there I visited {dungeon_name}. The entrance still bears marks of the old surveyors. I copied what I could and left the rest for someone with better eyes.

Use that template only for a ruin whose assigned local history includes survey marks and a fictional author recorded as visiting it. Otherwise use the neutral version:

> I reached {city_name} with mud on both sleeves. There is a ruin called {dungeon_name} in the country nearby. I have written its name here so that I cannot later claim I forgot it.

The book's writer and the player have separate histories. A journal's account never implies that the player has visited the same place.

# Monsters and unusual creatures

Status: draft proposal. The first three entries correspond to existing enemy kinds. Their prose can support future combat barks and bestiary pages. It does not change hostility, targeting, drops, or gate conditions.

## Crypt Skeleton

Content ID: `crypt_skeleton`. Existing enemy kind: `0`. Preferred placement: crypt chambers; allowed wherever the current generator places this kind.

Old duty workings keep some bones moving. One remnant counts beds. Another checks a door latch. A third guards a supply room long emptied. When interrupted, the remaining command treats a living visitor as a threat.

Use broken job instructions and occasional fragments of a former life. Keep barks brief. Most encounters need no speech.

Aggro: "Visitors must wait outside."

Attack: "Inventory discrepancy."

Hurt: "That was accounted for."

Rare hurt line: "There were beds here. I counted them."

Bestiary fragment: "A moving skeleton may still be carrying out a duty. Knowing its duty does not make approaching it safe."

Peaceful release or negotiation is a future mechanic. Do not tell the player that a password can unlock a gate or spare this enemy in the current build.

## Sanctum Invoker

Content ID: `sanctum_invoker`. Existing enemy kind: `1`. Preferred placement: violet study chambers; allowed wherever currently generated.

An invoker repeats the defensive lessons of the old atlas schools. Its remaining attention is devoted to completing the exercise. Nobody has told it that the examination ended generations ago.

Use formal lesson language. Let its fear of failing appear occasionally. Its speech should suggest a history without confirming whether every invoker was once human.

Aggro: "Identify your instructor."

Attack: "Demonstration follows."

Hurt: "The exercise is incomplete."

Rare hurt line: "Please. I practiced."

Bestiary fragment: "The sanctum taught its pupils to strike at an intruder from a distance. Whatever remains of the lesson still knows how."

## Tomb Shade

Content ID: `tomb_shade`. Existing enemy kind: `2`. Preferred placement: verdant memorial rooms; allowed wherever currently generated.

A shade is a lingering impression drawn into a hostile old working. Its speech holds moments rather than a complete autobiography. A memory of welcoming someone can become a demand that they remain forever.

Use intimate, incomplete statements. Do not give it administrative language from the skeleton or classroom language from the invoker.

Aggro: "You came back."

Attack: "Stay where I can remember you."

Hurt: "I had a name."

Rare hurt line: "Was it warm outside?"

Bestiary fragment: "A shade can remember being loved without remembering whom it loved. Its welcome is dangerous."

## Bell tortoise

Content ID: `bell_tortoise`. Future creature. Placement: Greenwood roadsides and meadow clearings. Baseline disposition: animal, usually peaceful.

Its hollow shell chimes as it walks. Family groups follow one another's rhythm. A solitary tortoise may follow a caravan bell for miles, then become distressed when the caravan leaves it behind.

Use shell sounds rather than human dialogue. Travelers can discuss its behavior. A future encounter could involve returning a lost animal to its group. The chime is a proposed biological feature, not a combat buff.

## Storm moth

Content ID: `storm_moth`. Future creature. Placement: Amber Wastes ruins with old inscriptions. Baseline disposition: animal; hazardous when disturbed.

It grazes on the faint charge left in enchanted stone. A disturbed cluster flashes bright enough to interrupt a traveler's sight. Traders wrap sensitive relics before passing a roost.

Use wing clicks and light. Do not give it a spoken quip. Blinding, feeding, weather prediction, and wrapped-relic protection all need future support.

## The Last Usher

Content ID: `last_usher`. Future rare hostile remnant. Placement: one authored or uniquely tracked atlas-office ruin, not every dungeon's final room.

The Usher still expects the delegates who approved the final atlas. It carefully sets out chairs, then removes them when it remembers that the guests cannot come. Its anger begins when someone tries to take a seat.

First warning: "Those places are reserved."

Hostile line: "I will not lose another guest."

Its history can connect to the Unclosed Office. Peaceful resolution, a boss encounter, or a unique reward must be designed by the coding agent before dialogue offers any of those outcomes.

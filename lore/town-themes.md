# Towns from other skies

From the field notes of a Milewarden

The worlds fell together. Their towns did not become alike. A carpenter still remembers the grain of a beam from a forest that stood beneath another moon. A baker keeps a feast whose winter no longer arrives on the same day. We record those things beside the roads, names and wells. They tell a traveler how to arrive kindly.

## Rainwood Street and Reedpaper Court

Rainwood's workshop fronts show narrow wooden lattices beneath dark roofs. Cloth menders work beside carpenters and letter writers. When rain catches a stranger, someone brings a dry cloth before asking for news.

At Reedpaper Court, a folded light hangs over the well. Bookbinders keep old letters even when the addresses belong to streets no one can find. A host asks whether a visitor wants company or a quiet corner. Either answer is welcome.

## Jadebeam Court and Porcelain Exchange

Jadebeam's joiners favor stepped brackets and carefully fitted pieces. They keep lists of sound timber left from earlier repairs. A good offcut may pass through three households before becoming the part for which it was best suited.

Porcelain Exchange gathers vessel makers and caravan households around a bright floating bowl. The market sells the things needed to make a guest comfortable. Ask how someone prefers their tea. The answer matters more than knowing the road they came from.

## Warmstone Village and Moonjar Commons

Warmstone families remember homes with stone rooms for cold days and airy wooden rooms for warm ones. Their present roofs carry quiet gray ridges. Guests find a place for their shoes before settling into the household room.

Moonjar potters leave slight differences between their white vessels. They say a town full of identical bowls would have forgotten the people who made them. Return a borrowed vessel with a little food inside, if you can.

## Harvestmask Hollow and Candlewake Borough

Harvestmask windows glow with little carved harvest faces. The masks are invitations to supper, even when they look alarmed, furious or terribly hungry. Borrow one if you like. A bare face is welcome at the table too.

Candlewake keeps a light for absent neighbors. People remember a bad joke, a favorite soup or a kindness that went unnoticed at the time. A visitor may offer a name. Listening quietly is also a way to join them.

## Evergreen Hearth and Giftmakers Square

Evergreen households kept their winter feast when the worlds collapsed. They hang green trim and small lights, bake what they can spare, and leave a meal ready for someone still on the road. Their welcome does not depend on a date in an old calendar.

At Giftmakers Square, repaired toys share benches with new ones. Makers let recipients decide what their gifts will become. Giving is a pleasure here, not a debt that every guest must repay.

## Neon Relay and Chrome Bazaar

Neon Relay glows with narrow lines of color against dark workshop walls. Its technomancers remember machines that once carried messages across their former world. They now work with etched plates, local magic and patient repair. Every encoded public notice has a plain copy beside it.

Chrome Bazaar trades conductive fittings and caravan components. Its repairers explain their patches before closing a casing. A visitor should leave knowing what was done to an object they depend on.

## Spellbinders Ward and Observatory Court

Spellbinders keep bright marks on their roofs and readable instructions beside their records. A household charm is a neighbor's work. Its maker should be able to explain what it does and who last repaired it.

Observatory Court collects charts of a sky assembled from many lost skies. The observers date every account. When a new chart disagrees with an old one, they preserve both and write down what changed.

## Dewbell Commons and Petalweave Court

Dewbell gardeners remember a world where flowers sheltered whole households. Their roofs wear small petal ornaments, and their well carries a shining drop. Ask before picking a bloom. A public path does not make every growing thing ownerless.

Petalweave households add colors to a pattern brought from their former world. They trade thread, flower dyes and small repairs. When borrowing, offer a repair you know how to make. A useful stitch is often remembered longer than a coin.

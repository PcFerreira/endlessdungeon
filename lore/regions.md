# Regions and places

Status: draft proposal. Placement: biome-specific conversations, travel books, ruin flavor, and future local encounters. Keep the generator's existing biome labels.

For physical placement, route composition, and shared site facts, see [World generation](world-generation.md). Its biome examples connect these customs to specific discoveries and state which interactions need implementation.

The [Area guide](areas-guide.md) expands these four biomes into a catalogue of ordinary and themed settlements, dungeon families, unusual districts, and transitions within the same continuous world.

## Greenwood

Foresters leave a strip of bark untouched when harvesting an old tree. They call it the tree's way home. Some trees bear copper survey nails placed before the Dawn, and their roots have grown around the inscriptions.

Ordinary life revolves around timber, honey, damp boots, and roads that need clearing. A local dispute concerns whether an ancient survey nail belongs to the town, the Milewardens, or the tree.

Optional local wonder: bell tortoises gather near a tree whose hollow trunk catches their shell chimes. Reserve the animals and tree interaction for future encounters.

Place travel journals at future roadside stops and forestry homes. Use foresters or Milewardens near city roads. Safe flavor line: "The oldest oaks still wear the surveyors' nails."

## Sunlit Meadows

Most towns hold a yearly feast for travelers who have not arrived yet. Plates go back into storage afterward. Households argue about whether their guests were late, lost, or never born.

Farmers, millers, and cloth merchants know that a bright field can hide old stonework. The ruins beneath one field may have belonged to an observatory; another may have been a burial house.

Optional local wonder: small wind spirits learn a farmer's whistled tune and repeat it around the scarecrow. This is a future encounter, with no current effect on crops or weather.

Place household books at future farm interiors or inns. Hearthkeepers fit near the fountain. Safe flavor line: "We set a place for whoever the road brings next."

## Amber Wastes

Caravan families mark cups with their owners' names and travelers carry cloth to shade water vessels. A full cup is a serious gift. Old glass fragments sometimes contain bubbles shaped like tiny letters, though nobody agrees whether they were made for reading.

People make a living transporting goods, repairing containers, and deciding which ruins are worth the journey. A cracked vessel can start a fierce argument about responsibility and restitution.

Optional local wonder: storm moths roost among exposed old inscriptions and gather before electrical storms. Their presence belongs to future creature and weather systems.

Place water manuals in future caravan shelters or town workshops. Potfolk would fit here once supported. Safe flavor line: "A full cup deserves a steady hand."

## Frostlands

Families wrap bells in cloth when a traveler is overdue. A bell can wait quietly for years. In the thaw, people repair bridges and read the names scratched into old milestones.

Communities share fuel and maintain shelter against the cold. A keeper may have excellent reasons for distrusting an adventurer who treats an old roof as firewood.

Optional local wonder: an echo returns a sentence spoken years ago. Use it in a book first. An audible world event needs a defined location and future implementation.

Place travel accounts at future inns, tower shelves, or sheltered ruin entrances. Safe flavor line: "Some bells are waiting for people, too."

## Sanctuary towns

Use the generated city name in dialogue. These customs vary among towns within the same biome. A town's assigned local wonder and dispute should remain stable once selected.

Keep fountain dialogue short and welcoming. A town can argue about relics while still providing the protection and recovery currently offered by sanctuary cities.

The existing enemy kinds can appear beyond their matching theme. Use theme as a preference for placement, not a claim that a creature cannot appear elsewhere.

An entrance name such as Whispering Crypt or Broken Sanctum is a generated label, not a unique historical monument. Give each ruin its own local history if that history will recur. Current dungeons have a finite floor count per entrance; the world's endlessness does not mean every individual dungeon goes down forever.
